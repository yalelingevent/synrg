The distributional restrictions on embedded root constructions form empirical and conceptual challenges to the subordination literature. This talk focuses on the non-standard embeddability profile of German embedded V2 (EV2) clauses (a.o., <a href="https://dx.doi.org/10.1515/thli.2002.28.1.33">Gartner 2002</a>, <a href="https://dx.doi.org/10.1515/TL.2006.018">Truckenbrodt 2006</a>) and argues for a Persp(ective)P(hrase) (a la <a href="https://dx.doi.org/10.1162/ling_a_00507">Dayal 2025</a>) analysis. I extend the Dayalian interrogative left periphery across another major clause type by proposing that the contrast between embedded V2 clauses and embedded dass-clauses boils down to a contrast between the declarative PerspP (PerspP<sub><i>p</i></sub>; <a href="#1b">1b</a>) and CP (<a href="#1c">1c</a>). The PerspP<sub><i>p</i></sub>, merged as the extended projection of the clause-typing CP<sub>[-Q]</sub>, (i) anchors the embedded <i>p</i> to a perspectival center via PRO in Spec, PerspP<sub><i>p</i></sub>, and (ii) introduces a not-at-issue requirement on the doxastic state of the perspectival center.
