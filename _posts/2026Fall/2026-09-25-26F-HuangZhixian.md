Mandarin argument linking exhibits surface flexibility: for some transitive verbs, the surface object position can alternate between (A) selected theme/patient arguments (cf.(<a href="#1">1</a>)) (B) unselected location/instrument arguments (dubbed ‘non-canonical objects’; Lin 2001 a.o.; cf. (<a href="#2">2</a>)) or (C) semantically inert objects (dubbed ‘dummy objects’; Cheng & Sybesma 1998, Badan 2013; cf. (<a href="#3">3</a>)). No two of A, B and C may co-occur.


<p id = "1">(1)</p>
<div data-gloss>
    <p>wo chi-le niurou-mian.</p>
    <p>I eat-PFV beef-noodle</p>
    <p>'I ate the beef noodle.'</p>
</div>


<p id = "2">(2)</p>
<div data-gloss>
    <p>wo chi-le shitang/kuaizi.</p>
    <p>I eat-PFV dining.hall/chopstick</p>
    <p>‘I ate at the dining hall.’/ ‘I ate using the chopsticks.’ (lit: ‘I ate the restaurant.’/ ‘I ate the chopsticks.’)</p>
</div>

<p id = "3">(3)</p>
<div data-gloss>
    <p>wo chi-le fan.</p>
    <p>I eat-PFV meal</p>
    <p>‘I ate.’</p>
</div>


In this paper, based on novel generalizations and diagnostics, I argue that this alternation is not free, but restricted to a subset of verbs that involve Caseless implicit arguments. The empirical theta role alternation is the theoretical reflex of the role of implicit arguments in mediating argument linking: when a theta role is saturated by an implicit argument that does not require Case, Case is freed to license additional arguments bearing alternative or null theta roles.
