<p>VP-ellipsis tolerates voice-mismatches between the antecedent and ellipsis site. The examples below contain an antecedent in active voice and an ellipsis site in passive voice.</p>

<p>(1) The janitor must take out the trash whenever it is apparent that it should be ⟨taken out⟩.</p>
<p>(2) The professor will assign a problem whenever it should be ⟨assigned⟩.</p>

<p>One account for the acceptability of the above sentences is that syntactic identity is enforced between VPs, and Voice is outside of the VP and therefore is not required to be identical (Merchant 2013). owever, I observe that voice mismatches in Antecedent Contained Deletion (ACD) configurations are degraded.</p>


<p>(3) *The professor will assign a problem that should be ⟨assigned⟩.</p>

<p>Passive-antecedent, active-ellipsis mismatch with an extraposed relative clause are similarly degraded.</p>

<p>(4) *Every proposal was rejected that the committee could ⟨reject⟩.</p>

<p>Assuming that voice-mismatch VP-ellipsis is grammatical and voice-mismatch ACD is ungrammatical, syntactic identity of VPs alone does not differentiate between typical VP-ellipsi and ACD and cannot explain why ellipsis is not licensed in (3). ACD forces a structure where the trace is bound by a higher binder, and this is the difference that blocks ellipsis in (3).

In this talk I locate the unacceptability of the ellipsis in (3) with the following ingredients.
i) A semantic identity condition that is sensitive to focus and involves Parallelism Domains (PD).
ii) ACD forces a larger PD than VP and constrains focus-marking.
iii) Active predicates are not semantically equivalent to passive predicates.

<p>However, voice-mismatches in ACD are sometimes acceptable:</p>

<p>(5) The professor didn’t assign every problem that should have been ⟨assigned⟩.</p>

<p>I propose that the ellipsis in this construction still does not have a viable direct antecedent, but that the addition of the negation and modal allows for an instance of Indirect Parallelism (Fox 1999), where an entailed proposition can serve an accommodate antecedent for ellipsis. Accounting for the data above requires an identity condition for ellipsis that is at least sensitive to structure (traces and binders in PDs) and semantic equivalence to an antecedent that can be accommodated.</p>
