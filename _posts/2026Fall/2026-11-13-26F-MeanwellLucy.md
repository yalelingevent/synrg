<p>In this talk, I revisit the basic nature of long-distance dependencies in Celtic, by investigating a puzzling preposition pied-piping pattern in Scottish Gaelic LDDs. A-bar extraction from a PP complement is distinct from basic DP-extraction in three ways: (i) the form of the complementizer takes a special form an, local to the pied-piped preposition, and the associated verb is in its dependent form; (ii) the pied-piped preposition follows its A-bar moved complement (inversion); and (iii) there is available the possibility of stranding the preposition in an intermediate Spec, CP (Adger and Ramchand 2006). I show that the Scottish Gaelic intermediate stranding data poses problems for current theories of pied-piping (Cable 2010), especially as they pertain to relativization, as well as stranding (Davis 2020). I then show that in conjunction with intermediate stranding, sensitivity to islands suggests these LDDs have the profile of a movement-generated dependency (cf. Sheil 2012, 2016), rather than that of null resumption (counter Adger and Ramchand 2005, Adger 2025). In light of these facts, I argue that the morphological sensitivity of the complementizer to the category of the moved XP in A-bar movement is evidence that PP pied-piping has a distinct profile from other types of pied-piping (Cowper 1987, Grimshaw 2000). I develop an approach to this pied-piping pattern that appeals to the involved complementizers attracting distinct categories (DP vs PP). Intermediate preposition stranding in Scottish Gaelic is thus a predicted by-product of the category difference between PPs and DPs.</p>
